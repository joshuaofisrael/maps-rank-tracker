How do service-area businesses rank on Google Maps?

By the RankFootprint team · Last updated 27 October 2026

Short answer: the same way as any other business: relevance, distance and prominence (Google Business Profile Help: Tips to improve your local ranking). Hiding your address doesn't hide where you're based, and listing a city in your service area doesn't make you rank there. Plumbers, cleaners, roofers and other businesses that travel to customers usually rank well near their base and fade further out, so the useful question is where that fade starts.

What the service area setting does

A service-area business visits or delivers to customers and doesn't serve them at its own address. A hybrid business does both. Google's rules (Google Business Profile Help: Manage your service areas):

SettingWhat Google says
AddressRemove it if you don't serve customers there; keep it if you're hybrid.
Service areasUp to 20, set by city, postal code or other area.
Overall sizeAbout 2 hours of driving time from where you're based, at most.

The setting describes where you work. Google's ranking guidance doesn't list it as a ranking factor (Google Business Profile Help: Tips to improve your local ranking), so adding every town in the county won't make you appear in all of them.

Why rank fades away from your base

Google still knows where a service-area business is based, even when the address isn't shown. For a searcher a few miles away, a competitor closer to them often wins on distance. Strong relevance (the right primary category, clear services) and prominence (reviews, links, local mentions) can push your reach further, but rarely evenly in every direction.

Rules that trip up service-area businesses

Don't use a virtual office, a mailbox or a co-working desk you don't staff as your address, and don't create extra profiles for each town you serve. Both break Google's guidelines and can get a profile suspended (Google Business Profile Help: Guidelines for representing your business). One real profile with an honest service area is the safe setup.

How to grow your reach in the towns you want

  • Pick the primary category that matches your main paid job, and list your real services.
  • Build a useful page on your website for each area you genuinely serve, with work you've done there, not copy-pasted city pages.
  • Ask happy customers in those areas for reviews, and reply to them.
  • Earn local mentions: suppliers, trade associations, local press, sponsorships.
  • Re-measure the same keyword from the same points every few weeks so you can see whether the edge of your reach is moving.

FAQ

Does adding more cities to my service area make me rank in them?

Not by itself. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. The service area tells customers where you work, but it isn't a switch that makes you rank everywhere you list.

Should a service-area business hide its address on Google?

If you don't serve customers at your business address, Google says to remove the address and enter only your service area. If customers do visit you, show the address and add a service area as a hybrid business.

How big can my Google service area be?

Google allows up to 20 service areas, and says the overall area shouldn't be more than about 2 hours of driving time from where your business is based.

Why do I rank near my base but not in the next town over?

Distance still counts even when your address is hidden. Google knows where the business is based, so rank usually fades with distance from that point unless strong relevance and prominence carry you further.
