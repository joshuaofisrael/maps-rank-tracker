Why does my Google Maps ranking look different when I search for myself?

By the RankFootprint team · Last updated 30 October 2026

Short answer: because you're not searching the way your customers do. You search from your own premises or home, on your own device and Google account, and Google uses all of that. Distance from the searcher is one of the main things behind local results, so checking from beside your own listing usually flatters you. Customers across town can see a different order. To get a fair picture, check from fixed points across the area you serve, the same way each time.

What changes the results you see

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and popularity (Google Business Profile Help: Tips to improve your local ranking). Its Search help also lists the context it takes into account, including location, language, device type and recent searches, and says signed-in users with personalized recommendations turned on may see results shaped by their search history (Google Search Help: Why your Google Search results differ from others). For a self-check, that adds up to several ways your view can differ from a customer's:

What's differentWhy it skews a self-check
Where you areYou're closest to your own business, so distance works in your favour. A customer two miles away may be closer to a competitor.
Your Google accountIf personalized recommendations are on, your own history (including searching for and clicking your business) can shape what you see.
Device and languagePhone and desktop results can be laid out differently, and language settings change which results suit you.
TimeGoogle notes that results can vary between searches a few hours or even minutes apart, as new content and ranking updates roll out.
Saved home and work addressesGoogle can use these to tailor local results, so your "neutral" search may still lean towards your own neighbourhood.

Why incognito doesn't fix it

A private window stops your signed-in history being used, which helps a little. It doesn't hide where you are. Google says your location comes from several sources used together to estimate where you are, and that it shows nearby results even when you don't type a place into the search (Google Search Help: Understand and manage your location when you search on Google). So an incognito search from your shop still shows the results for your shop's street, which is the one spot where you're most likely to look strong.

How to check your ranking fairly

  • Pick fixed check points. Choose spots spread across the area you actually serve, not just your own address. The free grid scan planner helps you lay them out, and choosing grid size and radius covers how far apart they should be.
  • Use the words customers use. Search for the service ("emergency plumber"), not your business name. Searching your own name tells you almost nothing about how new customers find you.
  • Keep the settings the same. Same keywords, same points, same device type and language every time, so changes reflect Google rather than your setup.
  • Look at trends, not one check. Results move day to day. Compare the same grid over several weeks before deciding something has changed. Our guide to reading a Maps rank grid explains what to look for.

If you'd rather do it by hand, how to check your Google Maps ranking in different areas walks through the free options, and why Maps rankings change across town explains the distance effect in more depth.

When your own check and a customer's disagree

Neither of you is wrong: you're both seeing what Google showed at that place and time. If a customer or client says they can't find you, ask where they were and what they typed, then check from that area. If you're missing from large parts of your service area, start with why a business doesn't show on Google Maps, and if a particular rival keeps beating you, see why a competitor is outranking you.

FAQ

Why do I rank higher on Google Maps when I search from my business?

Distance is one of the main things Google uses for local results, and you're searching from right next to your own listing. Customers a few miles away are further from you and often closer to competitors, so they can see a different order.

Does incognito mode show my real Google Maps ranking?

Not fully. A private window stops your signed-in search history being used, but Google still estimates your location from your device and network, so you still see results for where you're standing.

Why does my client see a different ranking than I do?

You're almost certainly searching from different places, and possibly with different devices, languages or account settings. Google says location, device type, recent searches and personalization can all change results, so two honest checks can disagree.

What's the fairest way to check my Google Maps ranking?

Check the same keyword from a set of fixed points across your service area, with the same settings each time, and compare the results over weeks rather than one day. That's what a grid rank scan does.
