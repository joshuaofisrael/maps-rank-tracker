How the numbers are worked out


We place a square grid centred on the business. Its width is twice the service radius, so a 3 mile radius gives a 6 mile square. The points are spread evenly from edge to edge, so the gap between neighbours is the width divided by the grid size minus one: spacing = 2 × radius ÷ (grid size − 1). The number of points is the grid size squared, and the area covered is the width squared. To convert miles to kilometres, multiply by 1.609.


How to plan a scan in four steps


  1. Measure your real service radius from your business address, the distance you'd actually travel for a job.
  2. Pick the spacing that suits the area: about 0.5 mi in a dense centre, 1 mi in a suburb, 2 to 4 mi for county-wide work.
  3. Find your radius in the table and choose the smallest grid that gets the spacing at or below your target.
  4. Run the scan with one keyword, then repeat it with the same grid and keyword each month so the maps can be compared.

Frequently asked questions
How many search points does a grid scan use?
A grid scan uses the grid size squared. A 5×5 grid is 25 points, a 7×7 grid is 49 points and a 13×13 grid is 169 points. Each point is one simulated search from that spot.
How do you work out the spacing between grid points?
Spacing is the full width of the square (twice the service radius) divided by the number of gaps between points, which is the grid size minus one. A 3 mile radius on a 7×7 grid gives 6 ÷ 6 = 1 mile between points.
Why does the square cover more than my service radius?
The grid is a square drawn around your business, so its corners sit about 1.41 times the radius away. The square's area is (2 × radius)², about 27% more than a circle of the same radius. Corner points may show as not found if you don't serve them.
What spacing should I aim for?
In our experience, about half a mile apart in dense city centres, around a mile in suburbs and towns, and two to four miles for county-wide trades. Closer points cost more searches but catch smaller changes.
