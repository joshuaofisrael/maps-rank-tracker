How should an agency report Google Maps rankings to clients?

By the RankFootprint team · Last updated 28 October 2026

Short answer: report rank as a map, not a number, and tie it to results the client can feel. Scan the same keywords from the same grid of points each month, show how much of the service area you rank in the top 3, and put that next to calls, direction requests and website clicks. Then explain what you changed and what you'll do next. Clients stay longer when they can see where they're winning and why.

Why a single rank number causes arguments

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and popularity (Google Business Profile Help: Tips to improve your local ranking). Distance means the same search gives different results a few streets apart. If you report "position 2" from the office and the owner searches from home and sees position 9, both of you are right, and the client stops trusting the report. A grid of check points across the service area removes that argument because it shows both answers on one map. Our guide to why Maps rankings change across town explains this for clients who ask.

Where the non-rank numbers come from

Verified Business Profiles have a performance report showing interactions, calls, direction requests, bookings, views on Search and Maps, and the search terms people used. Google notes that this data includes views and actions from both organic results and Google Ads, and that the searches metric updates at the start of each month and can take up to 5 days to appear (Google Business Profile Help: Understand your Business Profile performance & insights). Mention the Ads point in your report if the client also runs ads, so nobody credits paid clicks to SEO. For the website, the Search Console performance report gives clicks, impressions and the queries behind them (Search Console Help: Performance report (Search results)).

Keep every scan comparable

  • Use the same grid centre, size and spacing every month. Changing them makes old and new maps impossible to compare. The free grid scan planner helps you pick a grid once and stick with it, and choosing grid size and radius covers the trade-offs.
  • Track the same keywords, written the same way. Agree them with the client at the start, using the profile's search terms as a check.
  • Run scans at a similar time of the month, and note anything unusual, such as a profile edit, a suspension or a new competitor opening nearby.
  • Keep the old reports. Three or more months of the same grid shows a trend; one month mostly shows noise.

How to explain bad months honestly

Rankings fall for many reasons, and some are outside your control. Say what you saw, what you checked and what you're doing about it. Our guides on why a Business Profile ranking drops and why a competitor is outranking you give you a checklist to work through, and you can link clients to them directly. Avoid promising positions: no one outside Google controls local ranking, and a promise you can't keep costs more than a slow month.

FAQ

Should I send clients a single Google Maps rank number?

It's better not to. Map results depend on where the searcher is, so one number from one spot can look great or terrible depending on where it was checked. A grid of points across the client's service area, with the same points every time, gives a fairer picture.

How often should I report local rankings to a client?

Monthly suits most clients, because Maps rankings move day to day and short-term swings are mostly noise. Google also updates the Business Profile searches metric at the start of each month, so a monthly report lines up with that data.

What should a local SEO report include besides rankings?

Pair the rank grid with what the client actually cares about: Business Profile interactions such as calls, direction requests and website clicks, plus website clicks from Search Console. Rankings explain visibility; interactions show whether it turned into customers.

Can I promise a client a top-3 Maps ranking?

No one can honestly guarantee it. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and popularity, and nobody outside Google controls those. Report what changed and what you did, and set goals around trends rather than fixed positions.
